mnv4_audio.onnx is the A0 MobileNetV4-Conv-M student from the 2026-10-10
clean-data experiment. It was randomly initialized at the pruned p50 shape,
then distilled from EfficientAT MN10 on 6,311 FMA tracks whose recorded
licences are CC BY, CC0 or public domain (256.98 hours). No MPD preview audio,
playlist labels, previous student weights or generated audio trained A0.
Its 46 pointwise/projection operators use LatentJam Q4Conv1x1; other
convolutions are INT8. Export calibration uses 200 windows from the same
licence-filtered FMA training set. The contract is waveform [1,320000] to
embedding [1,960]. The teacher itself was pretrained on AudioSet; the
student's data filtering does not assert independent rights clearance of
the teacher's original training corpus.

universal_semantic_head.onnx is the A0-nosyn head: 27 logistic regressions
with freshly fitted coefficients, trained on clean FMA embeddings and
EfficientAT MN10 targets, plus the FMA genre labels. It uses no synthetic
clips and no MPD-fitted weights. The output order remains the 27-label
semantic_scores contract. The routing scores are not general probabilities
of a listener's preference. Both replacement weight files are licensed
Apache-2.0 by LatentJam. The SMART chain's learned score correction (12
weights in the app's code) was refitted on 2026-10-28 to rankings by local
Claude judges of the chain's own candidates in licence-filtered Free Music
Archive libraries; it no longer uses weights fitted on the Million Playlist
Dataset.

artist_knowledge.bin holds one product-quantized 384-dimensional vector per
artist of music_entities_250k.bin, so the two files ship together. Each vector
is the MiniLM text encoder's embedding (sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2,
Apache-2.0, run offline) of a one-line description of
the artist that DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash wrote from the artist's name alone; the
description text is not included. Each record also keeps a 16-bit fingerprint
of the artist's normalized name, up to two language indices, the middle of the
decades the model says the artist was most active in, and the model's own
confidence; the file keeps records only for the artists whose confidence the
app accepts. artist_adapter.bin is a two-layer network trained by LatentJam
that guesses such a vector from a track's metadata vector when the pack does
not cover the artist. It was trained only on metadata strings synthesized from
the same model's structured artist attributes (languages, decades, genres),
never on a listener's library. Its weights are 4-bit codes (groups of 32
rotated by a Walsh-Hadamard matrix, a Lloyd-Max codebook and an FP16 length
per group), quantized after training. DeepSeek's terms of use permit using its
outputs, including for training. The artist descriptions and attributes behind
artist_knowledge.bin and artist_adapter.bin are AI-generated content
(DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash).

music_entities_250k.bin is a hash index over artist names and aliases: the
250,000 MusicBrainz artists with the most artist credits, then musicians,
singers and groups from Wikidata that have a Wikipedia article and fall
outside that list, ranked by article count. MusicBrainz core data and Wikidata
are both CC0. The index stores only 40-bit name hashes and integer ids, never
the names themselves.

artist_colisten.bin lists, for about 82,000 artists of music_entities_250k.bin
with at least 50 listeners, up to ten artists with at least 50 listeners most
often listened to by the same people, each with an 8-bit similarity. It was counted offline from ListenBrainz listens (CC0,
https://listenbrainz.org), from listeners outside the evaluation sample; it
holds only entity ids and similarities, no listener data. It is offered under
CC0-1.0, like its source.

artist_pop.bin holds, for about 174,000 artists of music_entities_250k.bin,
ln(1 + the number of distinct ListenBrainz listeners) of the artist, counted
offline from ListenBrainz listens (CC0) of listeners outside the evaluation
sample; artists with fewer than ten listeners are left out. It holds only
entity ids and those values, no listener data. It is offered under CC0-1.0,
like its source.

artist_family.bin holds, for about 105,000 artists of artist_knowledge.bin,
the coarse genre family (classical, dance, pop, rap, rock, soundtrack) of the
first genre in the artist's knowledge-pack attributes, from the same offline
generation as artist_knowledge.bin. SMART uses it in place of a track's own
genre tag when the artist resolves.

artist_family_l2.bin holds, for about 184,000 artists of the knowledge pack,
the genre family on SMART's twelve-family taxonomy (classical, dance, pop, rap,
rnb, rock, metal, jazz, folk, reggae, latin, soundtrack), mapped from the
artist's pack genres. genre_families.json is that taxonomy: LatentJam's
phrase and suffix rules, plus a table of MusicBrainz genre names (CC0) mapped to
their nearest family through MusicBrainz's "subgenre of" relations. With the
current ranker a track's family is its own genre tag's on this taxonomy, and
the artist's only when the tag maps to none.

heads.json holds LatentJam's linear audio heads (currently instrumentalness):
weights fitted on the clean A0 track vectors of licence-filtered FMA tracks,
offered under Apache-2.0 like the encoder. SMART uses the head to keep an
instrumental soundtrack or classical queue instrumental.

smart_ranker.json holds the weights of a linear ranker LatentJam fitted on
the same ListenBrainz listens (CC0) to order SMART's candidate tracks; no
listener's library went into it.

smart_ranker_full.json holds the same kind of weights with the listener's own
history as features (heard, how often, how recently, which track followed
which); it was fitted on ListenBrainz (CC0) listens and reads only the history
kept on the device.

audio_tagger.bin is a small linear head that LatentJam trained to predict a
track's genre family and artist descriptor from mnv4_audio.onnx's embedding,
on Free Music Archive tracks licensed CC BY, CC0 or public domain only (FMA
metadata CC BY 4.0); its labels come from artist_knowledge.bin. It fills those
two fields for tracks whose tags and artist say nothing, flagged as imputed.
Its training data contains information from the Free Music Archive dataset
(Defferrard, Benzi, Vandergheynst & Bresson, "FMA: A Dataset For Music
Analysis", ISMIR 2017, https://github.com/mdeff/fma): the FMA metadata is
CC BY 4.0, and only tracks whose own licence is CC BY, CC0 or public domain
were used, each attributed to its artist in that metadata (fma_metadata,
tracks.csv). No FMA audio or metadata ships with LatentJam.

artist_lfm.bin holds one 32-dimensional vector (8-bit values and a scale) for
about 112,000 artists of music_entities_250k.bin: their item embeddings from a LightFM model (Apache-2.0
and MIT, run offline only; none of its code ships) that LatentJam trained on
which artists the same ListenBrainz listeners (CC0) play, plus each artist's
genre family, language and decade. It holds no listener data.

text_encoder.onnx is a three-layer, 256-wide BERT that LatentJam trained to
reproduce the mean-pooled sentence vectors of
sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2 (Apache-2.0,
https://huggingface.co/sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2); it contains
none of MiniLM's weights. text_vocab.txt is its own WordPiece vocabulary. The
training strings were artist names and aliases from the entity corpus above
(CC0), metadata strings synthesized from the DeepSeek artist attributes,
search-style phrases built from templates, MiniLM's vocabulary words, and
search phrases DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash translated into twenty languages. No
listener's library went into it. Its word embeddings are 4-bit blocks (ONNX
Runtime's GatherBlockQuantized), quantized after training, and its other
weights dynamic INT8.

No non-commercial model weights and no offline per-track descriptor table are
included in the application.
